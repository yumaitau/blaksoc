## Why this exists

blakSOC is built for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander community-controlled organisations and
Indigenous-owned businesses. Features that touch cultural data, consent and governance must be designed
with those communities. This folder holds how that happens and what was decided.

Guiding frameworks: the Maiam nayri Wingara Indigenous Data Sovereignty principles, the CARE Principles
(Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics), and Priority Reform Four of the
National Agreement on Closing the Gap.

## Draft terms of reference

### Purpose
The group advises Yuma IT on how blakSOC holds, protects, shares and reports on data about Aboriginal and
Torres Strait Islander people, Country and knowledge. Its advice is recorded in the
[decision log](decision-log.md).

### Membership (proposed seats, from issue #1)
At least one person from each of:
- an Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisation (ACCHO)
- a Prescribed Body Corporate or land council
- an Indigenous-owned business (for example, Supply Nation registered)
- an Indigenous IT or managed service provider

_To agree:_ the total number of members, terms, how members are nominated, whether organisations or
individuals hold seats, chairing, and conflicts of interest (for example, a member's organisation also
being a blakSOC customer).

### Remuneration
Members are paid for their time. _To agree:_ the rate, what counts as paid time (sessions, preparation,
review), travel, and payment method. **No rate has been set.**

### How advice becomes product decisions
_Proposed, to agree:_
1. Yuma IT brings a question, with the options and their trade-offs written in plain language.
2. The group discusses it. Members may take it back to their own organisations before answering.
3. The outcome is written as a decision-log entry and read back to the group before it is final.
4. Yuma IT records how each decision was built, or why it was not, and reports that back.
5. The group can revisit any decision.

_To agree:_ what happens when members disagree, and whether some decisions need every member's
agreement.

### Cultural safety and confidentiality
_To agree with the group:_ how restricted knowledge (for example gender-restricted material or
Sorry Business) is handled in sessions, who may attend, and what may be written down. The default is to
record less.

### Data from sessions
- Participation and recording need consent. See the [participant consent template](session-consent.md).
- Findings are de-identified before they leave the session notes.
- Participants can withdraw what they said until the summary is published.
- Raw notes stay with Yuma IT, are not used to train AI, and are deleted after an agreed period
  (_to agree_).
- A plain-language summary goes back to every participant before anything is published.
